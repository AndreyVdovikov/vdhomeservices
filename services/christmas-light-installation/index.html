Which Christmas lights are right for your house?

Christmas is closer than it looks. Most homeowners want a house that looks great without a big bill, and the first question is always the same: which lights, and how are they different? I'm Andrew, a handyman in Wesley Chapel. Here are the three options I see every season, what each one really involves, and who each one fits.

In this guideYour three optionsSide-by-side comparisonColors and real jobsCost calculatorWhich one to choose

Your three options

1. Store-bought lights (do it yourself)

You can walk into Walmart, Home Depot or any other store, buy a box of lights and hang them. It is cheap and quick, and at first glance there is no downside. In practice these lights usually come as one long string. It is hard to get the right length for your roofline, you have to climb up and clip every bulb yourself, and a full day can disappear on a ladder. The cost is the lights plus your time and effort.

Best for: homeowners who want to save money and are comfortable working at height.

2. Permanent lights (Govee and similar)

Permanent lights are installed once and stay on the house all year. You choose colors and lighting modes and change them for each holiday from a remote or an app, so nothing ever has to come down. The trade-offs: it is the most expensive option and it needs an experienced installer. The lights also need power from an outdoor outlet, so I check where that is available at the estimate. I install any permanent lights and can supply Govee lights.

Best for: people who want lights for every holiday and no yearly work.

3. Professional C9 installation

I come to your house, cut the cord to your measurements and attach the lights with clips made for shingles or gutters (and for tile and stucco). Nothing is damaged. You choose any professional C9 bulbs in any colors, and the lights can run along the whole roofline or anywhere else on the house. In January I come back, take everything down, pack it and store it for free until next year, or you can keep it yourself.

Best for: homeowners who want a clean professional look for the season without the work.

Which one to choose

It depends on you. If you are saving money and enjoy the work, store-bought lights will do the job. If you want a clean, professional look for the season and no January cleanup, professional C9 is the simple choice. If you want lights for Halloween, Fourth of July and everything in between, permanent lights pay off. I install both professional C9 and permanent lights, so I can tell you honestly which fits your house after a free estimate.

Questions

Good to know

How long does Christmas light installation take?

A typical house takes one to two days, and most are done in one day. Bigger or more complicated rooflines can take a second day.

Will the clips damage my roof, gutters or stucco?

No. I use clips made for shingles, gutters, tile and stucco, so your home is not damaged and the lights come off cleanly in January.

Can you install lights on a tile roof or a stucco house?

Yes. I attach lights to shingles, gutters, tile roof edges and stucco, so most Wesley Chapel and Tampa Bay homes work.

What happens to the lights after the holidays?

In January I come back, take the lights down, pack them and store them for free until next year. You can also keep them yourself.

Do I own the lights?

Yes. The lights belong to you, unlike with companies that rent them and take them back.

What colors can I choose?

Professional C9 bulbs come in 11 colors: pink, purple, yellow, cool white, warm white, pure white, green, teal, blue, red and orange. Mix them any way you like.

Do permanent lights come with a remote?

Yes, a remote is included at no extra charge.

What do permanent lights need for power?

They plug into a regular outdoor outlet at your house. I check where power is available during the free estimate.

When should I book?

As early as you can. Dates fill up as the holidays get closer, and a free estimate takes only a quick photo or visit.

How much does it cost?

Professional C9 installation is about $7 per linear foot, including installation, January takedown and storage. Permanent lights are about $8 to $12 per foot for installation plus about $2.50 to $2.90 per foot for the Govee lights. These are approximate prices. The exact number comes after a free estimate.
