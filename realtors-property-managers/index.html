Who this is for

One handyman for the handoff

When one family moves out and another moves in, the house usually needs a few days of small, boring, important work: holes patched, doors that stick adjusted, a missing closet rod put back, blinds hung, a TV mounted, a bed assembled. None of it is a job for a big contractor, and all of it blocks the closing, the key handoff or the next lease. That gap is what I fill.

I work with real estate agents and brokerages who need repairs finished before a listing goes live or after an inspection report comes back, with property management companies and landlords who turn over rentals, and with senior move managers, home care agencies and families who are moving an older person into a new home or a community. You send the address and the list, I do the work, and you get the property back in a state you can show, hand over or live in.

Move-out

Move-out and turnover repairs

After a tenant or seller leaves, I walk the property with your list or make one with you, then work through it room by room. The usual items are nail and anchor holes patched and spot-painted, scuffed walls touched up, interior doors adjusted so they latch, loose door knobs, hinges and cabinet pulls tightened or replaced, caulk redone around tubs, sinks and trim, damaged baseboards and trim fixed, torn vinyl or blinds replaced, closet shelves and rods put back, and burned-out bulbs swapped.

I also remove the leftovers of a tenancy that stop a place from showing well: wall-mounted TV brackets and shelves taken down with the holes properly patched, old curtain rods and hardware removed, and damaged items from the move-out inspection repaired instead of replaced when repair makes sense. If something needs a licensed trade, such as electrical, plumbing or HVAC work, I tell you straight away so you can call the right person and keep your schedule.

Move-in

Move-in setup and post-move work

The first week in a new home is when people need help most. I mount TVs and soundbars, hang pictures, mirrors and curtain rods, put shelves and floating shelves up, assemble beds, dressers, desks, wardrobes and patio furniture, install blinds, and set furniture where it belongs in each room. For new-construction buyers I handle the closing-day list: sticking doors, missing hardware, touch-up paint, caulking and small punch-list items the builder did not get to.

Agents often use this as a closing gift or a concierge service for buyers, and property managers use it so a new tenant walks into a home that is actually ready. Either way you get one phone number, a clear price before I start, and a finished list instead of half a job.

Real jobs

Work I have done for homes like yours

Inspections

Home inspection and pre-listing repairs

When an inspection report lands, the repair addendum usually has a short deadline and a long list of small items: loose handrails, missing outlet cover plates, sticking doors, gaps in caulk, damaged trim, loose fence boards and gate latches, minor drywall damage, stuck windows, cracked or missing screens and similar defects. I can go through the report with you, price the items I can do, and tell you which ones need a licensed contractor. For sellers, the same approach works before listing: fix the small things that photographs and showings punish.

Seniors

Moves for seniors and older adults

Moving a parent, client or resident is more than a truck and boxes. The new place has to be safe, familiar and ready the day they arrive. I hang family photos and artwork at the right height, mount the TV, assemble and place beds and furniture where the person actually uses them, change light bulbs in hard-to-reach fixtures, put up curtain rods and blinds, secure bookcases and tall furniture to the wall, and fix the small things that become real problems for someone living alone, such as a loose railing, a sticking door or a wobbly table.

I work calmly and without rushing, and I am happy to coordinate with the family, the move manager or the care coordinator so the home is finished before the move day, not after.

FAQ

Questions from agents and managers

Do you work with real estate agents and property management companies?

Yes. A large part of this page is for exactly that: agents who need repairs before listing or after an inspection, property managers who turn over rentals, and landlords with several properties. Text or call (656) 231-1836 with the address and the list.

What is included in a move-out or turnover repair?

Patching and touching up walls, adjusting doors, replacing loose hardware, caulking, trim and baseboard repairs, blinds, closet shelves and rods, bulbs and similar small items. Licensed trades such as electrical, plumbing and HVAC are not included, and I tell you early if the list contains them.

Can you do repairs after a home inspection?

Yes. Send the inspection report or the repair addendum and I will tell you which items I can handle, what they cost and which ones need a licensed contractor. This keeps the closing timeline moving.

Do you help with move-in, like TVs, pictures and furniture?

Yes. I mount TVs, hang pictures, mirrors and curtain rods, assemble beds and furniture, and place it in the rooms. Many agents and managers book this for the buyer or tenant on move-in day.

Do you help seniors and the agencies that move them?

Yes. I work with families, move managers and care agencies: pictures, TVs, furniture assembly, light bulbs, curtain rods, securing tall furniture and small safety fixes, so the new home is ready when the person arrives.

Can you work with a lockbox or when no one is home?

Yes, if you give me access details for the property in advance. I confirm when I arrive and when I leave and send photos of the finished work.

How do I get a price for a list of repairs?

Send the address and the list, with photos if you have them, by text or phone. I price the list as a whole so you know the number before I start.

Which areas do you cover?

Wesley Chapel, Land O' Lakes, Lutz, Zephyrhills, San Antonio, New Tampa, Tampa, Carrollwood, Brandon and Riverview, plus the rest of Pasco and northern Hillsborough County. If you manage a property nearby, ask.

Is there a minimum job size?

Small jobs are welcome. One or two items are fine, and a full turnover list is fine too. Call or text and describe what you have.

Have a closing, a turnover or a move coming up?

Text the address and the list. I will get back to you with a price.
