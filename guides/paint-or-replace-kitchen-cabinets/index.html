Paint or replace kitchen cabinets: how to decide

When a fresh coat of paint transforms a kitchen, and when new cabinets are the better choice. The decision, the painting process, and Florida humidity.

By Andrew · Updated November 16, 2026

The kitchen is the room people most want to change, and the cabinets are the biggest part of it. New cabinets can transform the space, but they are also the biggest expense and the longest disruption. Painting the cabinets you already have can give a very similar result for far less. I paint and renovate kitchen cabinets around Wesley Chapel, and this is how I help people decide.

The short answer

Paint if the cabinet boxes are solid, the layout works, and the real problem is the color and the dated look. Replace if the boxes are damaged or swollen, the layout does not work for you, or you are redoing the whole kitchen anyway. There is also a middle option: new doors and drawer fronts on the existing boxes, which fits when the boxes are good but you want a different style.

When painting is enough

Painting is the right answer when the boxes are solid, with no swelling, soft spots or broken parts, and the layout works for you, so you do not need more storage or a different arrangement. It also fits when you like the door style, say shaker, but not the color or the worn finish, when the budget or timeline does not allow a full remodel, or when you plan to sell or refresh the home and want the biggest visual change for the money. Many builder-grade kitchens in Wesley Chapel have solid wood or MDF cabinets in oak, honey or dark tones, and white, soft gray, navy or green paint takes them from dated to current.

When to replace

Replace when the boxes are swollen, warped or crumbling, which often happens from water damage near the sink or the dishwasher, or when the layout simply does not work, with too little storage, poor use of corners or the wrong sizes. It also makes sense if you are changing the counters, the appliances or the room layout at the same time, or if the cabinets are laminate or thermofoil that is peeling and cannot be painted well. If you are replacing the whole kitchen, my new kitchen installation page shows what that looks like.

The middle option, new doors and drawer fronts on the existing boxes, changes the style and is usually quicker and cheaper than all-new cabinets. It works well when the boxes are in good condition, and the new doors can bring a modern look and soft-close hardware at the same time.

How cabinet painting is done

The prep decides how long the paint lasts, so that is where the time goes. The doors, drawers and hardware come off and get labeled. Everything is deeply cleaned, because cooking grease is invisible and paint will not stick to it. Then I repair dents, holes and damaged edges, fill the old hardware holes if the hardware changes, and sand the surfaces so the paint has something to grip. A primer that suits the surface goes on next, which matters most on glossy finishes and on dark wood that bleeds tannins. After that come two or more thin coats of cabinet-grade paint with proper drying time in between, and finally the doors and drawers go back on, the hinges are adjusted, and any new hardware is installed. The floors, counters and appliances are covered the whole time.

Colors and hardware

White and off-white are timeless and make a kitchen look bigger. Gray and greige are soft and modern and easy to match with counters. Navy, green and black are bolder choices that often go on an island or on the lower cabinets, with white uppers, and a two-tone kitchen gives it depth. New handles and pulls make a big difference too. Pick the hardware finish, whether black, brass or nickel, before you choose the paint, so they work together, and look at the color next to your counters, backsplash and floor. Send me a photo and I will suggest options.

How long it takes

A typical cabinet painting job takes several days to a bit over a week, depending on the number of doors and drawers and the drying time between coats. We agree on the method and the schedule in advance so you know when the kitchen is back in use. Installing new cabinets takes longer, because it depends on the delivery of the cabinets.

Florida things to know

High humidity slows drying and can affect adhesion, so ventilation, the right products and enough drying time before the doors go back on all matter. Before painting I always check under the sink and around the dishwasher, because swollen boxes need repair or replacement first, and the grease near the stove has to come off completely. Cabinets next to a bright window fade in the sun, so a quality finish helps there too.

Common mistakes

Skipping the cleaning and sanding is the number one reason paint peels. Painting over water damage is the second, because the damage keeps spreading under the paint. Wall paint is not a substitute for a durable cabinet finish, hanging the doors back before the paint has cured causes sticking and marks, and choosing a color without looking at the counters, backsplash and floor leads to a kitchen where nothing matches.

FAQ

Is it worth painting kitchen cabinets?

If the boxes are solid and the layout works for you, yes. Painting changes the whole look of a kitchen for a fraction of the cost and time of new cabinets.

How long does a painted cabinet finish last?

A properly prepped and well-applied finish with good products lasts for years. Prep is what decides it: cleaning, sanding, primer and the right paint.

Can any cabinet be painted?

Most wood and MDF cabinets can. Cabinets with water damage, swollen boards, or peeling laminate or thermofoil need a closer look and sometimes repair first.

Should I paint or replace the doors only?

If the boxes are in good shape and you want a new style, new doors and drawer fronts on the existing boxes can be a good middle option, painted or stained to match.

How long is the kitchen out of use?

Painting usually means several days to a week or more, depending on the size of the kitchen and the drying time between coats. I give a schedule in the estimate so you can plan.

Do you also install new kitchens?

Yes. I install new kitchens, including cabinets, and handle the finishing. Plumbing, electrical and gas connections go to licensed trades.
