Paint or replace kitchen cabinets: how to decide

When a fresh coat of paint transforms a kitchen, and when new cabinets are the better choice. The decision checklist, the painting process, and Florida humidity.

By Andrew · Updated November 16, 2026

The kitchen is the room people most want to change, and the cabinets are the biggest part of it. New cabinets can transform the space, but they are also the biggest expense and the longest disruption. Painting the cabinets you already have can give a very similar result for far less. I paint and renovate kitchen cabinets around Wesley Chapel, and this is how I help people decide.

The short answer

Paint if the cabinet boxes are solid, the layout works, and the problem is the color and the dated look. Replace if the boxes are damaged or swollen, the layout does not work for you, or you are changing the whole kitchen anyway. A middle option, new doors and drawer fronts on the existing boxes, fits when the boxes are good but you want a different style.

When painting is enough

  • The cabinet boxes are solid, with no swelling, soft spots or broken parts.
  • The layout works for you and you do not need more storage or a different arrangement.
  • You like the door style (for example shaker) but not the color or the worn finish.
  • Your budget or timeline does not allow a full remodel.
  • You plan to sell or refresh the home and want the biggest visual change for the money.

Many builder-grade kitchens in Wesley Chapel have solid wood or MDF cabinets in oak, honey or dark tones. White, soft gray, navy or green paint takes them from dated to current.

When to replace

  • The boxes are swollen, warped or crumbling, often from water damage near the sink or dishwasher.
  • The layout does not work: not enough storage, poor use of corners, wrong sizes.
  • You are changing the counters, appliances or the room layout at the same time.
  • The cabinets are laminate or thermofoil that is peeling and cannot be painted well.
  • You want a different style that the existing doors cannot give you, such as flat-panel or full-height cabinets.

If you are replacing the whole kitchen, see my new kitchen installation page.

The middle option

New doors and drawer fronts on the existing boxes, painted or stained. It changes the style, and it is usually quicker and cheaper than all-new cabinets. It works well when the boxes are in good condition, and the new doors can add a modern look and soft-close hardware at the same time.

How cabinet painting is done

The prep decides how long the paint lasts. A proper job looks like this:

  1. Remove and label doors, drawers and hardware.
  2. Deep clean to remove cooking grease and residue. Paint will not stick to a greasy surface.
  3. Repair dents, holes and damaged edges, and fill the old hardware holes if the hardware changes.
  4. Sand to give the surface a grip.
  5. Prime with a primer that fits the surface, especially on glossy finishes and on dark wood that bleeds tannins.
  6. Paint two or more thin coats of cabinet-grade paint, with proper drying time between them.
  7. Reinstall doors and drawers, adjust the hinges and add new hardware if you want it.
  8. Protect the kitchen during the job: floors, counters and appliances are covered.

Colors and hardware

  • White and off-white: timeless, bright, and makes the kitchen look bigger.
  • Gray and greige: soft, modern and easy to match with counters.
  • Navy, green and black: bolder choices, often used on an island or lower cabinets with white uppers.
  • Two-tone: a different color on the island or the lower cabinets gives depth.
  • Hardware: new handles and pulls make a big difference. Pick the finish (black, brass, nickel) before choosing the paint color so they work together.

Look at the color against your counters, backsplash and floor. Bring a sample of those, or send me a photo, and I will suggest options.

How long it takes

A typical kitchen painting job takes several days to over a week, depending on the number of doors and drawers and the drying time. Spraying and brushing both work when the prep is right, and I will agree on the method and the schedule with you in advance. New cabinet installation takes longer, and depends on delivery of the cabinets.

Florida things to know

  • Humidity. High humidity slows drying and can affect adhesion. Good ventilation and the right products matter, and so does enough drying time before the doors go back on.
  • Water damage. Check under the sink and around the dishwasher before painting. Swollen boxes need repair or replacement first.
  • Grease. Kitchens near the stove collect grease, which must be removed before any paint goes on.
  • Sun. Cabinets by a bright window fade, so a high-quality finish helps.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the cleaning and sanding. The number one reason for peeling.
  • Painting over water damage. The damage keeps spreading under the paint.
  • Using wall paint. Cabinets need a durable, hard-wearing finish.
  • Not letting the paint cure. Hanging doors back too early causes sticking and marks.
  • Choosing the color alone. It has to work with the counters, backsplash and floor.

FAQ

Is it worth painting kitchen cabinets?

If the boxes are solid and the layout works for you, yes. Painting changes the whole look of a kitchen for a fraction of the cost and time of new cabinets.

How long does painted cabinet finish last?

A properly prepped and sprayed or well-applied finish with good products lasts for years. Prep is what decides it: cleaning, sanding, primer and the right paint.

Can any cabinet be painted?

Most wood and MDF cabinets can. Cabinets with water damage, swollen boards, or peeling laminate or thermofoil need a closer look and sometimes repair first.

Should I paint or replace the doors only?

If the boxes are in good shape and you want a new style, new doors and drawer fronts on the existing boxes can be a good middle option, painted or stained to match.

How long is the kitchen out of use?

Painting usually means several days to a week or more, depending on the size of the kitchen and the drying time between coats. I give a schedule in the estimate so you can plan.

Do you also install new kitchens?

Yes. I install new kitchens, including cabinets, and handle the finishing. Plumbing, electrical and gas connections go to licensed trades.
