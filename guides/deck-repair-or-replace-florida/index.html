Deck repair or replace: what to check first

Soft boards, loose railings and rotted posts: what is a simple repair, what means replacement, and what a Florida deck needs that others do not.

By Andrew · Updated November 16, 2026

Florida is hard on wood. Sun, rain, humidity, termites and a long season of heat break down decks, porches, stairs and railings faster than in most states. I repair decks and porches around Wesley Chapel, and the question I hear most is whether something can be repaired or has to be replaced. This guide gives you a check you can do in ten minutes, and tells you plainly where a repair ends and a job for a licensed contractor begins.

Why Florida decks wear out

Moisture is the main enemy. Daily rain in summer and constant humidity keep wood damp, especially near the ground and at joints. The sun dries out and cracks boards and strips stain and paint, termites and other insects go for soft or damaged wood, and cheap screws and nails rust and lose their grip. Add hurricane season, when wind and water loosen railings and posts, and you can see why a deck that looked fine last year can feel different this year.

The 10-minute inspection

Grab a flat screwdriver, a flashlight and a level. Start with the boards and look for cracks, splits, soft spots, nails or screws popping up, and boards that bounce when you walk on them. Then do the screwdriver test: press the tip into the boards, the post bases, the stair stringers and the rim joists. Good wood resists, and rotted wood gives way or crumbles. Check the bottom of every post, where it meets the ground or the concrete, because that is where rot starts.

Push on the railings. They should not move at all, and the balusters, the spindles between the posts, should be tight with none missing. On the stairs, each tread should be solid and level and the stairs should not shake. Then look at where the deck attaches to the house, which should be solid with no gaps, rust or water stains. If you see rot there, stop and get a professional opinion. Finally, look at the joists and beams from underneath for rot, termite tubes, sagging and rusted hardware, and lay a level on the surface, because a slope or a sag is a sign of structural trouble.

What can be repaired

Most of the jobs I do on decks and porches are repairs: replacing individual damaged boards, replacing rotted stair treads, fixing wobbly stairs, and tightening, rebuilding or replacing railings and balusters. I also replace rotted trim, fascia and small sections of framing, swap rusted screws and hardware for the right ones, and finish with cleaning, sanding and staining or painting. The rule of thumb is that if the structure underneath is sound, repairs are usually the better value. The deck repair page has more examples, and the wood rot repair page covers other damaged wood around the house.

When to replace

Replacement is the right call when the posts, beams or joists are soft or rotted and it is not just a single board, when the deck sags, slopes or bounces noticeably, or when the connection to the house is damaged. It is also the better choice when the rot or termite damage is so widespread that you would be replacing most of the deck anyway, or when the deck is old and the repairs cost more each year.

I am a handyman, not a licensed general contractor. Structural rebuilds, new decks and anything that needs a permit or an engineer's opinion should go to a licensed contractor. I will tell you honestly when that is the case, and what I can do in the meantime to keep the deck safe. Until then, keep people off a deck with soft posts, a sagging frame or a damaged connection to the house, do not lean on a loose railing, and fix wobbly stairs quickly, because falls happen there.

Materials

Pressure-treated lumber is the most common choice and the most cost-effective, though it needs sealing or staining and can crack and warp over time. Cedar and other woods look natural but cost more and need more upkeep. Composite boards last longer with little maintenance, but they cost more at the start and get hot in the Florida sun. Whichever board you pick, use screws and hardware rated for outdoor and treated-wood use, because that is what prevents rust streaks and loose boards.

Permits and HOA

Replacing boards, railings and trim is usually not a permit job, but rules vary by county and city, and new decks and structural rebuilds normally need a permit. If you live in an HOA community, check the rules on colors, materials and changes to the outside of your home before you start. When in doubt, call your county building department and your HOA.

Common mistakes

The worst one is painting over rot. It hides the problem for a few months and makes it worse. Replacing boards on a damaged frame is the same kind of mistake, because new boards will not hold on rotted joists. Indoor screws and nails rust and fail outside, a loose railing is the part that protects people and should never be ignored, and waiting is expensive, since small repairs cost far less than rebuilding after a year of water damage.

How to make it last

Clean the deck once or twice a year and clear the leaves and debris out of the gaps. Re-seal or re-stain on schedule, which in Florida sun is more often than the can says. Keep plants, soil and mulch away from the wood, check the posts and stairs after storms, and fix loose hardware early. If you want me to look at yours, send photos of the deck, the damage and the stairs, and I will give you a clear price before any work starts. FAQ

How do I know if my deck needs repair or replacement?

If the problems are limited to a few boards, a loose railing or worn stairs, repair is usually enough. If the posts, joists or the connection to the house are soft or rotted, or the damage is widespread, the structure may need replacing and a licensed contractor should look at it.

How can I check for wood rot?

Push a screwdriver into the wood in several places, especially near the ground, at post bases, board ends and where the deck meets the house. If it sinks in easily or the wood crumbles, it is rotted.

Is a loose deck railing dangerous?

Yes. A railing must hold a person's weight. If it moves or wobbles, do not lean on it and get it fixed. Loose railings are common and often a simple repair.

Can you replace only some deck boards?

Yes. Damaged boards can be replaced one by one, and the new boards weather to match over time. If the framing underneath is damaged, that needs to be fixed at the same time.

Do I need a permit for deck repair?

Replacing boards and railings is usually not a permit job, but rules differ by location and by HOA. Rebuilding the structure or building a new deck usually requires a permit. Check with your county and your HOA.

What wood is best for a deck in Florida?

Pressure-treated lumber is the most common and cost-effective. Composite lasts longer with less maintenance but costs more at the start. Whatever you choose, use fasteners rated for outdoor use.

How long does deck repair take?

Many repairs are done in a day. Larger repairs with posts or stairs take longer. I give a timeline with the estimate.
