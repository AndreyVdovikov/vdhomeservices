How much does a media wall cost in Florida?

Accent walls start from $1,500. A full media wall is typically $3,500 to $4,000, and large custom builds run $6,000 to $10,000 or more. Here is what drives those numbers and how to keep your project on budget.

By Andrew · Updated November 15, 2026

I build media walls and accent walls in Wesley Chapel and across Tampa Bay, and the first question is always the same: how much will it cost? The honest answer is that it depends, but not as much as you might think. Most projects fall into a few clear price ranges. This guide explains them, what moves the price up or down, and how to get an exact number without guessing.

The short answer

These are the ranges I work in. They are starting points for typical projects, not fixed prices:

  • Accent wall with TV mounting and a mantel: from $1,500.
  • Full media wall (a complete TV wall with shelves or cabinets, and a fireplace if you want one): typically $3,500 to $4,000.
  • Large custom project (big walls, built-ins, extra features and detail work): $6,000 to $10,000 or more.

For comparison, national cost guides put built-in entertainment centers at $5,000 to $15,000, with the average around $9,000. A media wall built from panels and trim, instead of full cabinetry, usually lands lower, because the work is mostly framing, paneling and finishing rather than custom furniture.

Three kinds of projects

1. Accent wall (from $1,500)

This is the fastest way to change a living room. One wall gets wood slats, paneling or shiplap, the TV is mounted level, and a mantel finishes the look. It looks like a designer touch and it is a simple project, often done in one or two days.

2. Full media wall ($3,500 to $4,000)

Here the whole TV wall is designed and built. It can have floating shelves, lower cabinets or both, a mantel, an electric fireplace and lighting. The wall is framed, finished and painted or stained to match your room. This is the most popular option in the new homes around Wesley Chapel, where the TV wall is often the largest bare wall in the living room.

3. Large custom project ($6,000 to $10,000+)

Floor-to-ceiling walls, full built-ins on both sides of the TV, custom workspaces, detailed millwork and integrated lighting. These take more design time and more days on site, and they are where the quality of the finish shows most.

What changes the price

  • Size of the wall. More width and height means more materials and more labor. This is the biggest factor.
  • Design complexity. Straight panels are simpler than shelves with different depths, niches or corners.
  • Shelves and cabinets. Floating shelves are lighter work than lower cabinets with doors and drawers.
  • Materials and finish. Painted MDF, plywood, solid wood and stained wood are all priced differently.
  • Fireplace. Adding a fireplace feature increases framing, finishing and sometimes electrical work.
  • Lighting. LED strips and accent lights add materials and detail work.
  • Wall condition. A flat drywall wall is simple. A block wall, an uneven surface or something that needs repair first takes more time.
  • Existing items. Removing an old unit, mantel or TV mount before the work starts is extra work.

The reason I ask for the wall width and height, and for photos, is simple: these two numbers plus a picture explain most of the price.

Materials

The most common materials for panels, slats and shiplap are:

  • MDF and paint-grade boards. Smooth, consistent and cost-effective. Perfect for painted walls in white, black or any color.
  • Plywood. Strong and stable. A good base for built-ins and shelves.
  • Solid wood. A richer, natural look with real grain. It costs more, and it is worth it for stained walls and floating shelves.
  • Wood slat panels. Vertical slats give a modern look and can be painted or stained in dark, light or natural tones.

I choose the material together with you based on the look you want and how the wall will be used. If you like a dark stained wall, solid wood or real wood veneer is the way to go. For a clean white or black wall, painted boards give the best value.

Adding a fireplace

A fireplace feature gives the room a warm focal point, and it is one of the most requested upgrades. Most of the media walls I build use an electric or linear electric fireplace. It looks like a real feature, it is built into the wall, and it does not need a chimney or gas line.

Two things to know. First, an electric fireplace needs a power outlet nearby. If there is no outlet in the right spot, a licensed electrician needs to add one. I do not do electrical wiring, but I plan the layout so the electrician knows exactly where the outlet must go and I build around it. Second, always follow the manufacturer's instructions for the clearance above the fireplace if you plan to hang a TV over it. Heat can damage a TV, so the height and the model matter.

Lighting and outlets

Warm LED accent lighting is a small addition that changes how the whole wall feels, especially in the evening. LED strips under shelves or behind panels make the wall glow softly and highlight your decor. Most LED strips plug into a standard outlet, so they do not need new wiring. If a design needs outlets moved or added behind the TV, that is again a job for a licensed electrician, and it is best planned before the wall is built, not after.

Florida things to know

  • Humidity. Florida air is humid, and unsealed MDF can swell. Quality painted finishes and sealing the edges protect the boards, and I use moisture-friendly materials where it matters.
  • Block walls. Many Florida homes have concrete block exterior walls. Mounting on block needs masonry anchors, and for a feature wall I often frame out the wall to make a flat, solid surface for the panels.
  • Bright rooms. Many Florida living rooms have large windows and lots of light. Plan the TV position and the finish of the wall so glare does not wash out the screen.
  • New-build homes. In communities like Epperson, Wiregrass Ranch and Meadow Pointe, a media wall is one of the most popular ways to personalize a house that came with plain white walls.

How to keep it on budget

  1. Start with what matters most. The TV, the mantel and the wall finish give the biggest visual change. Shelves, cabinets and lighting can be added in stages if the budget is tight.
  2. Choose painted boards over solid wood where the wall will be painted anyway.
  3. Decide on the fireplace early. Adding it later is more work than building it in from the start.
  4. Plan outlets and wiring first. Changes after the wall is built cost the most.
  5. Bring a reference photo. A picture of a wall you like removes guesswork and avoids redesign.

FAQ

How much does a media wall cost?

An accent wall with a TV mount and a mantel starts from $1,500. A full media wall with built-in shelves or cabinets is typically $3,500 to $4,000, and large custom projects run $6,000 to $10,000 or more. The exact price depends on wall size, design and materials.

What is the difference between an accent wall and a media wall?

An accent wall is a decorative feature wall, usually panels, slats or shiplap. A media wall is built around the TV and can include shelves, cabinets, a mantel and a fireplace. Many projects combine both.

How long does it take to build a media wall?

A simple accent wall is often a day or two. A full media wall usually takes several days, and a large custom build can take longer. I give you a timeline with the estimate.

Do I need an electrician for a fireplace or lighting?

If the design needs a new outlet or any new electrical wiring, a licensed electrician must do that part. I do not do electrical wiring, but I build the wall around it and plan the layout so the electrician knows exactly what is needed.

Is a media wall cheaper than buying a built-in entertainment center?

Often it is more cost-effective for a whole wall, because it is built to fit your exact space with no gaps. Ready-made units are cheaper for small spaces, but they rarely look built-in.

Can you match my existing colors and furniture?

Yes. Walls can be painted or stained to match your floors, furniture and trim. Send a photo of the room and I will suggest options.

What do you need to give me a price?

The width and height of the wall, a few photos of the room and, if you have one, a photo of a wall you like. You can send everything through the estimate form on the media wall page or text it to (656) 231-1836.
