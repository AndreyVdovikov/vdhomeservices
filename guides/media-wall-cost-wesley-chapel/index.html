How much does a media wall cost in Florida?

Accent walls start from $1,500. A full media wall is typically $3,500 to $4,000, and large custom builds run $6,000 to $10,000 or more. Here is what drives those numbers.

By Andrew · Updated November 15, 2026

I build media walls and accent walls in Wesley Chapel and across Tampa Bay, and the first question is always the same: how much will it cost? The honest answer is that it depends, but not as much as you might think. Most projects fall into a few clear price ranges, and once you know which one you are in, the number stops being a mystery.

The short answer

An accent wall with a TV mounted on it and a mantel underneath starts from $1,500. A full media wall, where the whole TV wall is designed and built with shelves or cabinets and maybe a fireplace, is typically $3,500 to $4,000. Large custom projects, the kind that run floor to ceiling with built-ins on both sides, go from $6,000 to $10,000 or more. Those are starting points for typical projects, not fixed prices, because every wall is a little different.

For a sense of the market, national cost guides put ready-made built-in entertainment centers at $5,000 to $15,000, with the average around $9,000. A media wall made from panels and trim usually lands lower, because most of the work is framing, paneling and finishing rather than building furniture from scratch.

Three kinds of projects

The smallest job is the accent wall. One wall gets wood slats, paneling or shiplap, the TV is mounted level, and a mantel finishes the look. It reads as a designer touch, it is a simple build, and it is often done in a day or two. This is where the $1,500 starting price lives.

The next step up is the full media wall, which is what most people picture when they say they want a media wall. The whole TV wall is designed and built: floating shelves or lower cabinets, a mantel, and quite often an electric fireplace and some lighting. I frame it, finish it, and paint or stain it to match the room. This is the most popular option in the newer homes around Wesley Chapel, where the TV wall is usually the largest bare wall in the living room.

Then there are the large custom builds, with floor-to-ceiling walls, full built-ins on both sides of the TV, workspaces, detailed millwork and integrated lighting. They take more design time and more days on site, and the quality of the finishing shows most here.

What changes the price

The biggest factor is simply the size of the wall. More width and height means more material and more hours, and it moves the price more than any other choice. Right behind size comes the complexity of the design. A straight run of panels is much simpler than shelves at different depths, niches or corners, and floating shelves are lighter work than lower cabinets with doors and drawers.

The finish matters too, since painted boards, plywood, solid wood and stained wood are all priced differently. A fireplace feature adds framing, finishing and sometimes electrical work, and lighting adds materials and detail work. The condition of the wall also counts: a flat drywall wall is easy, while a block wall, an uneven surface or something that needs repairs first takes more time. If an old unit, mantel or TV mount has to come off before we start, that is extra work as well.

That is why I ask for the wall's width and height and a couple of photos. Those two numbers plus a picture explain most of the price.

Materials

For painted walls I mostly use MDF and paint-grade boards, which are smooth, consistent and cost-effective, and they take white, black or any color well. Plywood makes a strong base for shelves and built-ins. If you want the look of real wood grain, like the dark stained wall below, solid wood or real wood veneer is the way to go, and it costs more. Vertical slat panels sit somewhere in the middle and can be painted or stained in dark, light or natural tones.

I choose the material together with you, based on the look you want and how the wall will be used. A clean white or black wall gives the best value with painted boards, and a rich stained wall is worth paying extra for real wood.

Fireplace and lighting

A fireplace gives the room a warm focal point, and it is one of the most requested upgrades. Most of the walls I build use an electric or linear electric fireplace. It is built into the wall and looks like a real feature, and it needs no chimney or gas line. It does need an outlet nearby, though. If there is none in the right spot, a licensed electrician has to add one. I do not do electrical wiring, but I plan the layout so the electrician knows exactly where the outlet must go, and I build around it. If you plan to hang the TV above a fireplace, always check the manufacturer's clearances, because heat can damage a TV.

Warm LED lighting under shelves or behind panels is a small addition that changes how the whole wall feels in the evening. Most LED strips plug into a standard outlet, so they do not need new wiring.

Florida things to know

Florida air is humid, and unsealed MDF can swell, so I seal the edges and use good paint, and I choose moisture-friendly materials where it matters. Many homes here also have concrete block exterior walls. Mounting on block takes masonry anchors, and for a feature wall I often frame the wall out first so the panels have a flat, solid surface. Bright rooms with big windows are common too, so it is worth thinking about glare before deciding where the TV goes. In new communities like Epperson, Wiregrass Ranch and Meadow Pointe, a media wall is one of the most popular ways to personalize a house that came with plain white walls.

Keeping it on budget

If the budget is tight, start with what changes the room the most: the wall finish, the TV and the mantel. Shelves, cabinets and lighting can be added in stages later. Painted boards instead of solid wood save real money wherever the wall will be painted anyway. Decide on the fireplace early, because adding it afterward is more work than building it in from the start. And plan the outlets and wiring before the wall is built, since changes after the fact cost the most. A reference photo of a wall you like also saves a lot of back and forth.

FAQ

How much does a media wall cost?

An accent wall with a TV mount and a mantel starts from $1,500. A full media wall with built-in shelves or cabinets is typically $3,500 to $4,000, and large custom projects run $6,000 to $10,000 or more. The exact price depends on wall size, design and materials.

What is the difference between an accent wall and a media wall?

An accent wall is a decorative feature wall, usually panels, slats or shiplap. A media wall is built around the TV and can include shelves, cabinets, a mantel and a fireplace. Many projects combine both.

How long does it take to build a media wall?

A simple accent wall is often a day or two. A full media wall usually takes several days, and a large custom build can take longer. I give you a timeline with the estimate.

Do I need an electrician for a fireplace or lighting?

If the design needs a new outlet or any new electrical wiring, a licensed electrician must do that part. I do not do electrical wiring, but I build the wall around it and plan the layout so the electrician knows exactly what is needed.

Is a media wall cheaper than buying a built-in entertainment center?

Often it is more cost-effective for a whole wall, because it is built to fit your exact space with no gaps. Ready-made units are cheaper for small spaces, but they rarely look built-in.

Can you match my existing colors and furniture?

Yes. Walls can be painted or stained to match your floors, furniture and trim. Send a photo of the room and I will suggest options.

What do you need to give me a price?

The width and height of the wall, a few photos of the room and, if you have one, a photo of a wall you like. You can send everything through the estimate form on the media wall page or text it to (656) 231-1836.
