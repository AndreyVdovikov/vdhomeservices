Accent wall ideas: slats, shiplap and panels

Slats, shiplap or panels? Which room, which color, how tall, and what to avoid in Florida's humidity. A practical guide with real projects from Wesley Chapel homes.

By Andrew · Updated November 16, 2026

An accent wall is the fastest way to change how a room feels without changing the furniture. One wall gets texture, color or pattern, and the whole room looks designed. I build slat walls, shiplap walls and panel walls across Wesley Chapel and Tampa Bay, and the questions I hear most are the same: which style, which color, which wall, and what holds up in Florida. This guide answers them.

Why an accent wall works

New-build homes in Wesley Chapel come with plain white walls and big open living rooms. A feature wall gives the eye a place to land, adds texture that paint alone cannot, and makes the room look finished. It also costs far less than redoing the whole room, and it is one of the few upgrades that changes the look of a house in a day or two.

Five popular styles

1. Vertical wood slats

Narrow vertical boards with small gaps, either stained to show the wood or painted. They draw the eye upward, so ceilings look taller, and they give the modern, warm look you see in newer homes. Slats are the most popular choice behind TVs and beds.

2. Shiplap

Horizontal boards with a thin shadow line. Painted white, shiplap is clean, bright and goes with almost any style. It is a safe choice if you may change furniture or colors later.

3. Board and batten

Tall vertical strips on top of a flat panel. Traditional and tidy, popular in dining rooms, entryways and bedrooms. It looks best painted in a deeper color.

4. Picture-frame molding

Rectangles of trim on the wall, painted one color. It gives a classic, formal feel and works especially well in dining rooms and hallways.

5. Solid wood plank wall

Wide wood planks, usually stained dark. It makes a strong statement, particularly behind a fireplace and TV. See the stained wall at the top of this page.

Which wall and which room

  • Living room: the wall behind the TV or the fireplace. It is where everyone looks anyway.
  • Bedroom: the wall behind the bed. Slats or board and batten look great here.
  • Dining room: the wall opposite the entrance, with picture-frame molding or board and batten.
  • Home office: the wall behind your desk, which is the one your video calls show.
  • Entryway: a short wall with a bench or hooks, the first impression of the house.

Choose a wall with few interruptions. A wall with several doors and windows breaks the pattern, and a plain wall shows it best.

Colors and finishes

  • White or light: bright and clean, ideal for smaller rooms and rooms with less light.
  • Black or charcoal: dramatic and modern, and it makes a TV blend into the wall.
  • Natural or light stain: warm and calm, good with light floors.
  • Dark stain: rich and bold, a strong match with dark furniture.
  • Muted green, navy or gray-blue: popular deeper colors for bedrooms and dining rooms.

Tip: look at your floors, furniture and the light in the room before choosing. A dark wall in a room with little light feels heavy, and a very light wall in a bright room can look washed out. Send me a photo of the room and I will suggest options.

Accent wall behind a TV

This is the most common request. A few points make it work:

  • Plan the TV position first, at a comfortable eye level. See the TV mounting guide for heights.
  • Add a mantel or shelves under the TV so the wall looks finished and has a place for decor.
  • Think about the cables before the wall is built. Hidden runs are far easier at this stage.
  • For a fireplace feature or a full wall with shelves, see the media wall cost guide.

Florida things to know

  • Humidity. Florida air is humid. Unsealed MDF can swell, so edges and cut ends need proper sealing and good paint. Where the wall is near a door to the lanai, I pay extra attention to this.
  • Block walls. Many homes have concrete block exterior walls. A feature wall on block is framed out first, which makes a flat, solid base.
  • Outlets and switches. A paneled wall adds thickness, so outlets and switches must be brought forward. Any change to the wiring itself is for a licensed electrician.
  • Light. Large windows and strong sun change how colors look through the day. Check the color in the morning and the evening.

What it costs

An accent wall with a TV mount and a mantel starts from $1,500. Larger walls, shelves, cabinets, a fireplace and lighting move the price up. The full breakdown is in the media wall cost guide. For an exact number, send the wall width, height and a photo.

Common mistakes

  • Too many accent walls. One feature wall is a feature. Three are a clash.
  • Picking the color from a tiny sample. Try a larger sample on the wall and look at it at different times of day.
  • Ignoring outlets and switches. They need to be moved forward, and it is easiest to plan before the panels go up.
  • Skipping the TV plan. Mount height and cables should be decided first.
  • Using unsealed boards. In Florida humidity this is what leads to swelling and peeling.
  • Visible nails and rough corners. The finishing is what makes the wall look expensive.

How the job goes

  1. Send photos and the wall size and the style you like.
  2. Estimate. You get a clear price and timeline before anything starts.
  3. Prep. The wall is measured, and furniture is moved or protected.
  4. Build. Framing where needed, panels or slats fixed in place, the TV mount and mantel installed.

FAQ

What is the most popular accent wall style right now?

Vertical wood slats and white shiplap are the two I build most. Slats give a modern, warm look. Shiplap is clean and fits almost any home. Board and batten and picture-frame molding are popular in dining rooms and bedrooms.

How much does an accent wall cost?

Accent walls with a TV mount and a mantel start from $1,500. The final price depends on the wall size, the style and the finish. Send the wall width, height and a photo for an exact price.

Which wall should be the accent wall?

Usually the wall you see first when you walk into the room, or the wall behind the TV or the bed. Avoid walls with many doors and windows, because the pattern gets broken up.

Is it better to paint or stain a wood accent wall?

Stain shows the real wood grain and gives a rich, warm look. Paint gives a solid color and hides the grain. Stain looks best on solid wood or real wood veneer, and paint is the better value on MDF boards.

Will an accent wall work on a block wall?

Yes. On a concrete block wall the panels are fixed to a frame, which also makes the surface flat. It adds a little depth, so outlets and switches are moved forward with the wall.

How long does it take?

A simple accent wall is often one or two days. Walls with shelves, a fireplace or detailed finishing take longer. I give a timeline with the estimate.

Can you add lighting to an accent wall?

Yes. Plug-in LED strips behind shelves or along the panels work well. Any new wiring must be done by a licensed electrician, and I plan the wall around it.
