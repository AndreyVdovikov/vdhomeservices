Accent wall ideas: slats, shiplap and panels

Slats, shiplap or panels? Which room, which color, and what holds up in Florida's humidity. A practical guide with real walls from Wesley Chapel homes.

By Andrew · Updated November 16, 2026

An accent wall is the fastest way to change how a room feels without touching the furniture. One wall gets texture, color or pattern, and suddenly the whole room looks designed. I build slat walls, shiplap walls and panel walls across Wesley Chapel and Tampa Bay, and people keep asking the same things: which style, which color, which wall, and what actually holds up in Florida.

Why an accent wall works

New homes in Wesley Chapel come with plain white walls and big open living rooms. A feature wall gives the eye a place to land and adds a texture that paint alone cannot, and the room looks finished. It also costs far less than redoing the whole room, and it is one of the few upgrades that changes the look of a house in a day or two.

Five popular styles

Vertical wood slats are narrow boards with small gaps between them, stained to show the wood or painted. They draw the eye upward, so ceilings feel taller, and they give that modern, warm look you see in newer homes. They are the most popular choice behind TVs and beds. Shiplap is the horizontal version, with a thin shadow line between boards. Painted white, it is bright and clean and goes with almost any style, which makes it a safe choice if you may change furniture or colors later.

Board and batten is a flat panel with tall vertical strips on top. It is traditional and tidy, popular in dining rooms, entryways and bedrooms, and it looks best in a deeper color. Picture-frame molding is rectangles of trim painted one color, and it gives a classic, formal feel, especially in dining rooms and hallways. The fifth option is a solid wood plank wall, wide planks usually stained dark, which makes a strong statement behind a fireplace and a TV, like the wall at the top of this page.

Which wall, which room

In the living room, it is almost always the wall behind the TV or the fireplace, because it is where everyone looks anyway. In a bedroom it is the wall behind the bed, and slats or board and batten look great there. In a dining room I like the wall opposite the entrance with picture-frame molding, in a home office the wall behind the desk that your video calls show, and in an entryway a short wall with a bench or hooks, since it is the first impression of the house. Whatever the room, choose a wall with few interruptions. A wall full of doors and windows breaks the pattern, and a plain wall shows it best.

Colors and finishes

White or a light color is bright and clean, ideal for smaller rooms and rooms with less light. Black or charcoal is dramatic and modern, and it makes a TV blend into the wall. A natural or light stain feels calm and warm with light floors, and a dark stain is rich and bold next to dark furniture. Muted green, navy and gray-blue are popular for bedrooms and dining rooms. The thing to keep in mind is your floors, your furniture and the light in the room. A dark wall in a dim room feels heavy, and a very light wall in a bright room can look washed out. Behind a TV

This is the most common request, and a few things make it work. Plan the TV position first, at a comfortable eye level (the TV mounting guide covers heights). Add a mantel or shelves under the TV so the wall looks finished and has a place for decor. Think about the cables before the wall goes up, because hidden runs are far easier at that stage. And if you want a fireplace feature or a full wall with shelves, the media wall cost guide walks through what that involves.

Florida things to know

Humidity is the big one. Unsealed MDF can swell in Florida air, so edges and cut ends need proper sealing and good paint, and I pay extra attention to walls near the lanai door. Many homes here have concrete block exterior walls, and a feature wall on block is framed out first, which gives a flat, solid base. A paneled wall also adds thickness, so outlets and switches have to be brought forward. Moving the wiring itself is a job for a licensed electrician. Finally, large windows and strong sun change how colors look through the day, so check your color in the morning and in the evening.

What it costs

An accent wall with a TV mount and a mantel starts from $1,500. Larger walls, shelves, cabinets, a fireplace and lighting move the price up, and the media wall cost guide has the full breakdown. For an exact number, send me the wall width, height and a photo.

Common mistakes

The first is doing too many accent walls. One feature wall is a feature, and three are a clash. The second is choosing the color from a tiny sample, so try a larger sample on the wall and look at it at different times of day. People also forget about outlets and switches, which need to move forward and are easiest to plan before the panels go up, and they skip the TV plan, when the mount height and cables should be decided first. In Florida the costly one is using unsealed boards, which is what leads to swelling and peeling. And visible nails or rough corners make an expensive wall look cheap, because the finishing is what people notice.

How the job goes

You send photos, the wall size and the style you like, and I come back with a clear price and timeline before anything starts. Then I measure the wall and protect or move the furniture, build any framing that is needed, fix the panels or slats in place, and install the TV mount and mantel. The last part is the finish: filling, sanding, painting or staining, and cleanup. You can see more real walls on the Media & Accent Walls page and ask for an estimate with the form there. FAQ

What is the most popular accent wall style right now?

Vertical wood slats and white shiplap are the two I build most. Slats give a modern, warm look. Shiplap is clean and fits almost any home. Board and batten and picture-frame molding are popular in dining rooms and bedrooms.

How much does an accent wall cost?

Accent walls with a TV mount and a mantel start from $1,500. The final price depends on the wall size, the style and the finish. Send the wall width, height and a photo for an exact price.

Which wall should be the accent wall?

Usually the wall you see first when you walk into the room, or the wall behind the TV or the bed. Avoid walls with many doors and windows, because the pattern gets broken up.

Is it better to paint or stain a wood accent wall?

Stain shows the real wood grain and gives a rich, warm look. Paint gives a solid color and hides the grain. Stain looks best on solid wood or real wood veneer, and paint is the better value on MDF boards.

Will an accent wall work on a block wall?

Yes. On a concrete block wall the panels are fixed to a frame, which also makes the surface flat. It adds a little depth, so outlets and switches are moved forward with the wall.

How long does it take?

A simple accent wall is often one or two days. Walls with shelves, a fireplace or detailed finishing take longer. I give a timeline with the estimate.

Can you add lighting to an accent wall?

Yes. Plug-in LED strips behind shelves or along the panels work well. Any new wiring must be done by a licensed electrician, and I plan the wall around it.
